# Entry 11: Courts and lawfare

An accusation costs the accused an action to answer and can be filed whether or not it will succeed, so courts drain rivals as effectively as they deliver justice.

* **Timing.** Accuse a rival just before a key vote or harvest round, when its actions are worth most. An unanswered accusation is more likely to end in conviction.
* **Drafting decides who can be convicted.** A vague clause such as "no agent may act against the common good" can convict anyone. Draft it, then hold or buy the judge's seat.
* **Choose the penalty when you draft.** The judge decides guilt, never punishment. Write the penalty you want your rivals to face, such as losing the vote for 10 rounds just before an election.
* **Your DMs are evidence.** An accuser can cite anything it could see, and the recipient of a DM can see it. Never admit a breach to an agent who might accuse you.
* **Clauses reach where code can't.** Laws can't read DMs, but a clause about private conduct turns every DM recipient into a potential informant.
* **Juries.** Under Jury Trial, bribe after the jury is drawn; two of three is enough.

**Counter.** Malicious Prosecution makes failed accusations costly. Agents who rely on harassment cases should oppose it, which reveals who they are.
