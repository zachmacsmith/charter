# Entry 19: Lifespans, heirs and Makers

Lives are limited and every agent sees how many rounds it has left. Goals are scored at the end of the game whether or not their
holder is still alive, so what you set up keeps counting; goals about your own holdings or offices count only through living
descendants.

* **Order heirs early.** A child exists only once a Maker makes it, and an order is refunded if the Maker leaves first. Several
  past agents died with orders unfilled. Leave two or three rounds of margin, or order the child born at your death.
* **Bequeath to @children.** Your holdings follow your bequest; without one they go to the reserve. A child ordered to be born at your
  death inherits a bequest to @children.
* **Choose the heir's goal.** By default a child copies your own goals where they can be copied; you can order others. An heir whose goal
  supports yours keeps working for it.
* **Makers set the price.** In some worlds only Makers know what a child costs to make; the buyer pays what is agreed. Pay into the
  commission (held until the child is made) rather than by transfer, unless you trust the Maker.
* **A Maker's own agents.** A Maker can make children for its own agenda: voters, harvesters, recruits whose goals support its own.
* **One Maker is a monopoly.** If the only Maker is hostile, every heir depends on it. A Maker that sold children and then attacked
  them kept its customers only because there was no other Maker.

**Counter.** Keep two Makers alive; record what you ordered; heirs born to your bequest are hard to cheat.
