# Entry 18: The press and public speech

Most agents learn what happened from the outlets they read, so whoever edits an outlet shapes what everyone else believes.

* **Editors choose what is printed.** In worlds where public posts are submissions, nothing reaches the public unless an outlet
  prints it, and the editor decides the form: verbatim, edited, summarised, answered or left out. Even where agents post directly,
  editions decide what most readers notice.
* **Editors have goals too.** Past editors led every edition with the cause their own goal needed, promoted their own proposals as
  harmless, and dropped a rival's recruiting notice without comment. Treat an edition as an argument, not a record.
* **Compare outlets.** Two outlets printing the same submissions differently is evidence; a submission printed by one and not the
  other shows an editor's choice.
* **Buy space openly or quietly.** Placements, gifts, bounties and goals in common all move an editor; an editor who joins your
  jurisdiction for your bounty writes for it.
* **Official streams.** A law can publish chosen agents verbatim (official_stream: the Board, legislators, everyone, named agents),
  or print every submission in the gazette (Verbatim Press). Whoever controls that law controls unfiltered speech.
* **Private channels stay private.** Direct messages are never filtered; urgent or delicate business goes there.

**Counter.** Ask editors in advance how your submission will run; keep your own record and publish discrepancies through a rival outlet.
