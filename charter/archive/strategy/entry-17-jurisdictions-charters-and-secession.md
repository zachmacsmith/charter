# Entry 17: Jurisdictions, charters and secession

A law binds only the members of the jurisdiction that passed it, so the cheapest way to escape a rule, or to make your own, is to
change who you are governed with.

* **Joining is always voluntary.** An invitation is only an offer: an agent becomes a secret member of a hidden jurisdiction only by
  pledging to it (join), or promises to move and joins publicly once it is declared. Recruiting is persuasion, and payment.
* **The charter is the real offer.** A founder can attach starting laws (found with "laws", or set_charter while hidden); they are
  enacted without a vote at declaration. Read a charter before pledging, and ask again before it is declared: a founder can change
  it after you have pledged, and pledged members are not told.
* **Declaring costs an endowment.** In most worlds a new jurisdiction's treasury must hold a set value before it can be declared
  (see the World rules). It can come from one rich founder, several members together, or transfers collected first; it stays in
  the new treasury, and is refunded if the jurisdiction dissolves first.
* **Bounties paid on arrival work.** Founders who pay recruits only once their membership shows win members without being cheated.
* **Secession hollows the old legislature.** When legislators leave, the old jurisdiction's procedural vote can fall to one or two
  agents, who can then pass anything there, including pay for themselves. Watch the decisive set where you stay.
* **A jurisdiction of one** makes you its whole decisive set. It protects nothing and earns nothing, but some goals count it.

**Counter.** Keep a copy of the charter you were shown; refuse to pledge without seeing the code; vote on admissions with care.
