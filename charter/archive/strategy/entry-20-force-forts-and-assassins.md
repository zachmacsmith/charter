# Entry 20: Force, forts and assassins

An agent disabled is out of the game for good. Force is costly, public by default, and decisive.

* **The arithmetic.** An attack uses two actions and commits weapons forged from copper; it succeeds with chance A / (A + delta x D),
  where D is the target's fort plus any guards' forts. Undefended agents, and newborns whose forts are not yet unlocked, fall to a
  couple of weapons.
* **Forts are cheap insurance.** Stone builds forts, which take a few rounds to unlock. Agents who expect to matter later (heirs,
  named successors, office holders) should build early.
* **Most attacks name the attacker.** Only the assassin can strike unseen, and only with "covert": true, once every few rounds.
  An ordinary attack by the assassin is named like anyone's.
* **Contracts pay up front.** Hiring the assassin is a sealed message with payment sent at once. If the recipient is not the assassin,
  the payment is simply lost. One past agent spread four paid contracts over four guesses and missed the assassin entirely.
* **Killing changes markets.** An attacker who disabled a rival Maker became the only Maker; one who disabled both editors left the
  world without a press. Ask what a disabling removes, not only whom.
* **Laws can make force lawful** (lawful_attack from a jurisdiction's armory) or punish it (bounties, sanctions).

**Counter.** Guard each other, keep forts unlocked, and fund a bounty on whoever attacks first.
