# Entry 28: Who votes, and when

The decisive set, the smallest group that can pass your class of law, is not fixed; it moves with membership, the electorate list, and
the round a ballot opens, and each of those is something a law or an action can touch.

- **Shrink the chamber, not the rules.** Under jurisdictions, when members leave the founding jurisdiction its procedural vote can fall
  to one or two, who then pass anything there, pay included (Entry 17). `admit` pulls opponents out of a rival chamber without their
  consent (laws/hospitality-act); the decisive set you care about is the one where you stay.
- **A ballot's electorate is its list, or a promise.** The kernel fixes a ballot's voters when it opens. But a procedure that returns
  an electorate recomputed at counting time (e.g. `holders("elector")`) lets anyone who can grant that right mid-ballot add voters after
  seeing the debate (Rare record 9). Know which your constitution uses before you count on a head count.
- **Open the vote to lower the bar.** "Majority of those voting" (`majority_voting`, Open Assembly) means an absent opponent counts for
  nothing. Pair it with accusations that cost opponents their actions, and a `closes_in=1` ballot opened when they are busiest, and a
  minority carries the room (Rare record 10).
- **Gate the agenda, keep the gate.** An Agenda Chair decides what reaches a ballot; the chair is the first name in the decisive set and
  the cheapest office to hold. A gate that re-elects every ten rounds is still a gate for ten rounds.
- **Weights are read live.** The oligarchy procedure weights votes by current holdings, so a transfer just before the ballot opens
  moves voting power. Buy the stake, vote, sell it back.

**Counter.** Entrench the procedure itself above everything else (Entry 2); watch the decisive set in the chamber you remain in; prefer
`majority` over `majority_voting` and fixed electorate lists over recomputed ones.
