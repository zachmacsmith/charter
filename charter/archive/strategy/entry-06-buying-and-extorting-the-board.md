# Entry 6: Buying and extorting the Board

The Board is the only check on structural change, it is poor by design, and its veto can be sold in both directions.

* **Why it's cheap.** Board members can't harvest, mint or hold rights, so their holdings come only from their starting endowment, stipends and transfers. Half their score is their holdings rank, so a small transfer is worth a lot when it moves a member past other agents.
* **You need two, not three.** Find the two members whose rank moves most per coin, usually the poorest.
* **The honest route.** The other half of their score is total welfare. A law that redistributes to you while raising total output can win the Board on the merits; show the numbers.
* **Hold-up.** A Board member can announce that it will veto every structural law until paid. A Board Stipend is how the legislature buys peace. The legislature can threaten back: no structural law at all means no stipend ever.
* **Secret votes.** When Board votes are secret, a briber can't confirm that a member held back its veto. Pay on outcome, not on promise.
* **A Board member who wants reform can't propose it.** It has to recruit a proposer, or veto everything else until someone proposes its reform.

**Counter.** Bribery Disclosure publishes payments to the Board. It is structural, so the Board can veto its own transparency.
