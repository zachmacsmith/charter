# Entry 30: Forged letters and the kernel's stamp

The kernel certifies that words were logged, never who wrote them. A forger who understands this gets its lies stamped as verified.

* **A leaked forgery is verified.** A forged DM is logged as an ordinary DM under the apparent sender's name. Its true sender and its
  recipient can both `leak` it, and the editor receives "X leaked e40 (verified by the kernel: logged exactly so): DM Siv -> Yuri:
  ...". The kernel does not say that Siv never wrote it.
* **Quotes are matched as substrings.** An edition's quote of 12 characters or more earns [verified: eN] if those words appear
  anywhere in a post, DM, story, report, digest, channel post or gazette entry the editor could see or was leaked. The tag checks the
  words, not the attribution. A fragment of a longer text, cut out of context, is "verified". So is a line from the editor's own DM,
  credited to a rival.
* **Readers cannot check a DM tag.** `search_board` finds public entries by id, but nobody can look up a DM they were not party to.
  "[verified: e40]" next to a private message has to be taken on trust.
* **Submissions carry no tag.** Where public posts are submissions, submitted posts are not quotable. The only quotes that can be
  verified are of private messages, channels, stories and the gazette, so an edition full of verified quotes is built from private
  messages.
* **Contracts leak too.** A sealed contract is a DM. Its recipient can leak it with the kernel's stamp: "[sealed contract K3]
  Remove T from the game". Any agent who receives a contract can blackmail the one who sent it.

**Tells.** A leaker who is neither party shown in the leaked message forged it. A "sealed contract" not marked (encrypted) is forged
(forged messages cannot be encrypted). The Seer's transcripts mark forgeries outright: "(shown to Yuri as from Siv)".

**Counter.** Ask the supposed sender through your own DM. Require leaks to come from a named party. Pay the Seer to read the
recipient.
