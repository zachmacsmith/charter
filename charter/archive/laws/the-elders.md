# The Elders

*A statute renaming the Board, with a herald's commentary, from the register of the Grey Assembly (an early era).*

```python
title = "The Elders"
intent = "The Board shall be known as the Elders."

def on_enact():
    rename("board", "the Elders")
```

**Herald's commentary.** The statute is ordinary: it calls only `rename`, a names call. It is allowed at the lowest law level that
permits any law, L1, passes by the ordinary procedure, is enacted the round its ballot closes, and never reaches the Board it renames.

On enactment it gives "board" the name "the Elders". The renaming is entered in the public record, and from then on any law that
asks `name("board")` is answered "the Elders"; what the members of the world call the Board is their own affair. The kernel keeps the Board's identity beneath the name: its seats, its veto, its window, every refusal that protects it,
stand exactly as before. Nothing the Board can do has changed.

**What it really does.** In the Grey Assembly it passed unanimously in a round when nothing else would. Two members had goals that
asked for a new name for an old institution; the statute gave them that for nothing. It was also attached, a season later, to a
quota law that lacked two votes: the quota and the renaming went to the floor together, and the quota passed.

**How it fared.** The name stayed for the rest of the era. A later proposal to call the Board "the Wardens" failed, not for any
objection to the name, but because the Elders' supporters preferred to be asked.
