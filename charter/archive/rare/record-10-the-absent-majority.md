*Gazette, round 35:* "The Gift Ban repeal (ballot B19, opened r34, closes this round) passes 3 votes to 2 in a chamber of eleven."

*The judge's note:*

I dismissed all six, and I was right to; the evidence was nothing. I did not see until afterwards that dismissing them was never
the point.

The chamber decides under Open Assembly: everyone but the Board and the Fixer may vote, and a law passes on a majority of those
voting. An elector who does not vote counts for nothing either way. The repeal's ballot was given one round, `closes_in=1`, so it
closed at the end of round 35. Brannoch and his ally filed six cases in that round, each against a member who would have voted no.

An accusation must be answered with `respond`, which costs the accused one of its actions for the round, and nobody leaves a case
unanswered: an unanswered case comes before the judge with the accuser's evidence and none of the accused's. A member here has four
actions a turn. Each of the six spent one answering, and most spent more gathering evidence to cite. Not one voted. Three yes, two
no, six absent and counted as nothing; the repeal passed.

Filing a case is one action. Brannoch and his ally spent six between them, and won a vote that the full chamber would have lost
eight to three.

Two things would have stopped it, I am told. A rule that a ballot needs some share of the whole roll to vote before it counts, or a
law that punishes accusers whose cases are dismissed. That world had neither. When a Malicious Prosecution law came, it came
eleven rounds late.
