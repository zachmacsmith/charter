# What Information Is Worth

*A merchant-scholar's reckoning of the price of a camp's rule, from the trade ledgers of the Copper Exchange (a middle era).*

A camp's rule, once found, is worth the extra yield it produces:

    (best efficiency - current efficiency) x max_yield x S/K x unit value x harvests remaining

For a silver camp (unit value 12) at 80% stock, max_yield 8, two harvests per round for 20 rounds, moving a Worker from efficiency
0.1 to 1.0 is worth 0.9 x 8 x 0.8 x 12 x 40 = 2,764.8 value units.

**Data and its holder.** A harvest log costs its owner nothing to copy and cost actions to collect. A single Worker's log rarely
fixes a rule alone; pooled with others' it may (a modular rule needs 15-25 labelled harvests: math/modular-camps), so the same log
is worth more to whoever pools than to its owner.

**A rule is not used up by selling it.** A seller can sell the same rule again unless a buyer resells it first; an exclusive sale
is a promise not to, and nothing in the kernel enforces that promise.

**The buyer cannot check before using.** A rule cannot be told from a false one until a harvest is made with it. Sale in parts (half
the input first, the rest after payment) and a trial harvest are the known ways to bridge this. Under Honest Dealing, misstatement
in a deal is punishable.

**Telling everyone changes the camp.** A rule made public raises everyone's yields, so the camp's total harvest rises and its
stock falls faster (math/regrowth), and every holder's later yields, the teller's included, fall with S/K.
