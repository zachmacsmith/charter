# Auctions and pricing rights

When rights are auctioned (Licence Auction), each bidder should bid at most the value of what the right produces over the period:
harvests per round * rounds held * expected yield * unit value, minus the cost of learning the camp.

- First-price sealed bids: bidding your full value earns nothing; shade your bid below it. With n similar bidders, bidding about
  (n - 1)/n of your value is the textbook equilibrium.
- Winners' curse: the bidder who most overestimates the camp tends to win. Knowledge of the yield function (efficiency) is what
  makes a right worth more to one agent than another.
- Proceeds go to the reserve: an auction raises P for any reserve-backed currency, so coin holders gain from it.
