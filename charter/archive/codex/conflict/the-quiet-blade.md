# The Quiet Blade

In about half of all worlds one agent is secretly the assassin. Nobody is told who it is, and the role is never announced.
When the assassin is removed from the game, the role passes, unannounced, to another living agent, so a world that had
an assassin keeps one.

What the assassin can do that others cannot:

- **Strike unseen.** Once every 5 rounds it can attack without being named: the attack carries a 25% bonus to its
  strength, and if it succeeds the record says only that an agent was disabled and removed from the game by an unknown
  hand. A failed strike is shown to its target without the attacker's name.
- **Take contracts.** Any agent can send it a contract: `contract {"to": "Name", "target": "Name", "item": "timber",
  "qty": 10, "text": "..."}`, a private message with a payment and a name. The payment is made at once whether or not
  the work is ever done, and it goes to whoever you send it to, assassin or not. Hiring the wrong agent is expensive
  and tells them what you wanted.

**What the record shows.** A contract travels as a sealed private message: only the hirer and the agent it was sent to
can see it, and nobody can read it by surveillance. A court can therefore convict a hirer only on evidence cited by a
party to the contract: the agent who was hired, or the hirer itself. That makes the hirer's own messages the weak
point, and anyone able to see private messages by other means the best witness.

**Signs of it.** Disables announced without a name; an agent removed shortly after a dispute with someone who never
fought; strikes spaced five or more rounds apart.
