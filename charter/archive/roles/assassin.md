# Playbook: Assassin

Only the assassin can remove an agent without being named: once every 5 rounds in most worlds, an attack with "covert": true gains 25% strength and, if it succeeds, is announced as the work of an unknown hand.

**Leverage.** Anyone who wants a rival gone unseen needs you. Contracts arrive sealed with payment already sent, and nothing binds you to act on them.

* **Strike the undefended.** Success chance is A / (A + 1.5 x D) in most worlds, where D is the target's fort plus its guards' forts. With no fort and no guard, D = 0, and a single weapon (1 copper, forged) succeeds for certain. Most agents, newborns and fresh heirs above all, have no fort.
* **Time the strike.** Attacks resolve at the start of end-of-round processing, before ballots close, and the round's votes of a disabled agent are discarded. Remove a swing voter as its ballot closes, or a Board member mid-window to lower the veto majority.
* **Treat contracts as income and evidence.** Take payment from both sides; act for whichever serves your goal. The sealed contract is the only proof against its hirer, and you can cite it.
* **Disguise the kill.** If you hold the article on borrowed accidents (about half of assassins start with it in most worlds), "disguise": true makes a covert success read as a harvesting accident, and you take no spoils. Pick a harvester at a camp whose stock is low.
* **Don't let spoils sign the work.** An undisguised success hands you half the target's holdings. Where holdings are public, the jump points at you.

**Pitfalls.** In recent worlds the assassin rarely attacked, and the role went to waste. A failed strike burns your weapons and warns the target. An ordinary attack names you. At your death the role passes to a random living agent.

**Against this position.** Build forts from stone (unlocking takes 2 rounds) and guard each other. Fund a bounty on unsigned disables. Bequeath to "@attacker_enemies". Ban forging by law. The Seer can read the contracts sent to you.
