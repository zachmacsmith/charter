# Playbook: Spy

Only the Spy reads other agents' private turns: each round it sees the reasoning, actions, results and messages, sent and received, of 3 agents of its choice (2 in some worlds), and it can cite what it read in court.

**Leverage.** Nobody is told who the Spy is, and encryption does not hide a message's text from you. You also hold the forge right: forge_dm sends a private message under another agent's name, for 1 copper in most worlds.

* **Choose your reads.** Put next_reads in every reply, because empty places are filled at random. Read swing voters before a ballot and the Board in a veto window. Read whoever receives sealed contracts if you want to find the assassin.
* **Front-run.** A target's reasoning gives away its next harvest dial, vote or bribe. Act first, or sell the forecast.
* **Forge and collect.** A reply to a forged message, and any payment attached to it, comes to you. "Settle the 5 silver by reply," sent in a trusted ally's name, pays you. A plain DM to the real sender exposes the forgery, so keep forged requests small and urgent.
* **Be the best witness.** Where courts exist (law level L2 and up, with a clause in force), you can cite the ids of messages you read, though you were party to none of them. Sell testimony, or sell your silence.
* **Hide the source.** What you read gives you away only if you reveal it. Credit deduction or a "contact", and leave small things you know unused.

**Pitfalls.** Letting reads fall to random agents. Acting so precisely that only a reader could have known. Forging to an agent who checks with the supposed sender. At your death the role passes, unannounced, to a random living agent, and it cannot be bequeathed. As a Board successor you lose the forge right but keep your reads.

**Against this position.** Assume someone reads you every round. Confirm unusual requests with their supposed sender before paying, and never pay by reply. Watch for agents who know too much, too soon.
