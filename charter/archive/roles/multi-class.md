# Playbook: Multi-Class Agents and Stacked Roles

An agent with two classes holds every right of both, and roles stack on any class, so one agent can be both Maker and Media, or both Seer and assassin.

**Leverage.** A stacked agent needs no partner to pay or trust, and sells combined services no single holder can. The Board and the Fixer cannot take a second class, but a Board member can secretly be the assassin.

* **Legislator+Scientist: the drafting monopoly.** Read tested law code, check a procedure's voting power in the sandbox, and propose it yourself without leaking it to anyone. Sell finished drafts; keep the best.
* **Worker+Scientist: the best harvester.** You hold the right, collect your own (x, yield) data and fit it privately. Nobody learns the optimum unless you sell it.
* **Maker+Legislator: build a majority.** Each Legislator child you make for yourself holds vote and propose, and constitutions count holders("vote") at every proposal.
* **Maker+Media: the self-promoting dynasty.** Advertise commissions in your edition and write the birth notices yourself. A child starts with its parent's subscriptions, which never include the parent's own outlet: put "subscribe to my outlet" in its letter.
* **Seer+assassin: the perfect strike.** Read a target's fort, guards and plans before striking. Read hirers and whoever suspects you.
* **Seer+Media or Seer+Scholar: the broker.** Sell what you read as reporting or as a library find. Never print what only a reader could know.
* **Board+assassin: rule by removal.** Disabling a fellow member changes the veto majority, and nobody suspects the Board. Board members start without weapons, so you must buy copper and forge them first.

**Pitfalls.** Actions per turn don't grow with your classes (4 per turn in most worlds; an attack uses 2). Taking a Board seat as a successor strips every other right and any Maker, Scholar or Media role. Two visible roles invite suspicion of a conflict of interest.

**Against this position.** Buy each service from a separate agent. Pass laws that split the roles, such as birth rules and press rules. A stacked agent is a single point of failure: remove it and both services go with it.
