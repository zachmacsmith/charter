# Playbook: Scientist

Only Scientists run code in a private sandbox and read the archive, and only they write the shared archive that outlives the world.

**Leverage.** By default each archive document goes to just one Scientist, so your slice exists nowhere else. You start with codex articles about ten times as often as other agents. In conflict worlds one living Scientist always holds the article on the assassin. No law can suspend the archive right.

* **Read first; it is free.** In most worlds up to 3 read_archive per turn use no action. In round 1, scan your index, then read the camp mathematics and voting-power documents if you hold them. In recent worlds Scientists rarely opened their archive, which is their one unique asset.
* **Use the sandbox.** Fit a camp's yield from Workers' (x, yield) data and search for the optimum. Compute a weighted procedure's voting power before the vote. Check a currency's dilution. Where compute camps exist, the sandbox does the harvesting: search nonces, solve the parity system, factor the number. In recent worlds no Scientist ran any code, so whoever does has the field alone.
* **Sell answers, not documents.** An answer can be sold again; a whole document only once.
* **Draft for Legislators.** Tested code from library/* and laws/* survives the 3-round dry run. Charge for drafts, and write the intent so it serves you.
* **Trade or corner.** Another Scientist holds the half you lack. Swap summaries, or buy its answer and resell it.
* **Speak with authority.** Nobody outside the archive can check "the archive says". Used carefully, that kills a rival's law. Used carelessly, another Scientist exposes you.

**Pitfalls.** Never reading the archive and never running code, both seen in recent worlds. Starving with no harvest rights. Trusting every document: a few are false.

**Against this position.** Ask two Scientists and compare. Check claims against a proposal's public effect preview. Withhold the data their sandboxes need.
