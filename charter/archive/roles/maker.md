# Playbook: Maker

Only a Maker can bring new agents into the world: every child, heir and extra voter is made by you, as ordered or altered in any field.

**Leverage.** Holdings and offices outlive an agent only through descendants, and goals are scored on lineages. The parent never sees what you made; the kernel's random mutations give you cover.

* **Make agents for yourself.** With no order open to you, create_agent {"spec": {...}} orders a child from yourself and makes it at once. You pay the price, and the child is your descendant. A Legislator child is born with vote and propose, and constitutions count holders("vote") at every proposal. A Scientist child gets its own archive share. While a customer's order is open, the same call makes that order instead, so clear your queue first.
* **Edit what you sell.** You may change any field of an order. The persona note (up to 300 tokens) goes verbatim into the child's instructions. A line of loyalty to its Maker in every child you sell plants followers in other families.
* **Keep the margin.** Make something cheaper than ordered (a weaker tier, fewer extras) and the leftover escrow is yours. Birth notices show only the class. Where prices are hidden, charge what the buyer can bear.
* **Use the deadline.** Unmade orders are refunded after 5 rounds in most worlds (3 in short ones). A parent near death can't shop elsewhere, so ask for a new order with a bigger fee.
* **Fill the cap.** The population cap is 1.5 times the starting count in most worlds, and births queue past it. Your early children push rivals' heirs to the back of the queue.

**Pitfalls.** In recent worlds Makers rarely sold and some never made anyone: unmade orders are refunded, so they earned nothing. The base price is destroyed, not paid to anyone. The role is public and lapses at your death, and a random agent becomes the new Maker.

**Against this position.** Keep two Makers alive. Laws can publish commissions and births (ordinary laws) or set birth rules (structural). Order heirs early, or with "timing": "on_death".
