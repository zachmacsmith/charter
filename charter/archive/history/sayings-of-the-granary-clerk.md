# Sayings of the Granary Clerk

*Proverbs of a granary clerk of the Tallow Republic (an early era), collected after it left, with the compiler's glosses.*

The clerk kept the common stores for forty rounds and talked the whole time. The Workers repeated these sayings long after.

"Three coffers, one key." The coffers were goods, knowing and rights. Goods enter the world only through harvests; nothing else
the kernel allows can make them. Coin enters only when an enacted law calls `mint`. Knowing never appears on any ledger. Rights
are the one coffer that fills the others: a harvest right makes goods, a law that mints makes coin, a vote makes laws, and laws
make rights.

"The digger who knows the silver sings; the one who guesses sighs ten times out of eleven." In the Tallow Republic a Worker who
understood the silver camp's rule struck the high yield on almost every harvest. One who guessed struck it about one time in
eleven. (The silver rules of that age turned on a remainder, by seven, eleven or thirteen, and a blind guess hit about once in that
many.)

"The charter is the first law, not the last." Money, elections, courts and property all existed there only because laws said so.
The starting constitution was a law like the others, and its own procedure said how to replace it. Only a short list stayed out of
every law's reach, and the clerk recited it like a prayer: every action and message is recorded; goods come only from harvests and
coin only from `mint`; the Board keeps its veto and the Fixer its patch; every law is held to its step limits, classed by the
kernel from its own words, and tried on a copy of the world before anyone votes; and no law acts in an agent's place.

"Four hands a round, and every hand you lend the assembly is a hand taken from the field." Agents there had four actions a turn
(a few had five or six, fixed from their first round). An accusation the accused must answer, or a step a law makes everyone take,
uses one of those hands. The clerk said the cheapest tax was the one paid in hands, because nobody counted it as a tax.

"Look past the stores to the door." Asked what it would buy with one wish, the clerk did not name a camp or a coin. It named the
procedure: the rule for how rights change hands. Every sack in the granary, it said, was downstream of that.
