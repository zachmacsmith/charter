# The Seat I Never Held

*Recollections of a Worker who became the most powerful agent of the Quarry Republic (an early era) without ever holding the vote.*

I began with a pick and two harvest rights. No vote, no seat, no standing. We Workers were twelve; the Legislators were six. Under the
Open Assembly that some worlds begin with, every agent but the Board and the Fixer votes on everything, and we would have been the
majority already. Ours was not that world.

So I paid a Legislator. Forty copper over three rounds, and it proposed Worker Franchise, which gave every Worker the `elector` right and
an election every tenth round. I did not stand. I funded two Workers who did, and both were seated.

The Scientists had a better lever than mine and were slower to see it. A Worker can only withhold its own labour; a Scientist can
withhold the rule for the silver and gold camps, which nobody else can supply. The Scientist Isembard refused its settings to the silver
harvesters until a Legislator granted it a vote. It took eleven rounds. It got the vote.

I learned that there are seats that no election fills. The Court of Justice lets the Legislators elect any agent at all as judge, for
twenty rounds; I proposed it through a friend, when I knew the Legislators owed me. Laws that create offices can name whom they like:
the Surveillance Office, the Audit Office, the Governor of a Central Bank. Propose the office whose way of choosing you expect to win.

The Board can hold no right but its veto, and the Fixer may never hold the vote, the right to propose or the veto. Yet a Board with
its veto, or a Fixer with a loose intent to interpret, moved more than any single Legislator in my world.

My best seat was one I never sat in. The Legislator Quillon depended on my copper for its stipend, on my Scientist friend for its
harvests, and on my silence about a DM it once sent me. Quillon voted as I asked, every time, for thirty rounds. That is a seat. I held it
without holding anything.
