# Accounts of a Number-Seller

*A Scientist's letter to her former partner settling their accounts, found in the archive of the Brass Concord era.*

Lysander,

You asked where my copper comes from now that I hold no camp right. Here are the accounts. You may judge them as you like.

**The reactor.** Each round the kernel posts a new batch code. The catalyst number is the smallest n for which the hash of the code and
n begins with three zeros. It takes thousands of tries, a moment in the sandbox and impossible by hand. A harvest without the number
yields about a tenth, so the number is worth nine tenths of every harvest. And the number is the same for every holder of the camp in a
given round. I compute it once and sell it to all five Workers. Each of them writes `"credit": "Clemence"` into its harvest, and at
the end of the round the camp pays me thirty parts in a hundred of each catalysed harvest. Nobody has to trust anybody.

**The station.** Each reading there is a weighted sum of the settings, plus noise. Whoever submits the exact weights shares the claim's
fifth of the pool with any other correct claimant. In our season the rest was split equally among everyone who had taken readings, so
a single reading bought an equal share of four fifths of the pool. I took one and gave my numbers to Tobiah, who solved the weights.
Last season the split went by the number of readings, and Orrin padded his share with cheap repeated readings at the same settings.
They added nothing to the solution, but they counted. Every claim uses a harvest, so in the round we solved it I submitted two
candidate vectors at once, in case the rounding had gone wrong in one of them. I told nobody the weights until both claims were in.

**The booth.** The target there is a fixed fraction f of the average of all entries, and the entry closest to it takes the pot, one
unit for each entrant. With fewer than three entrants nobody wins. Three of my friends entered 100. I entered fT / (N − f), where N
is the number of entrants and T the sum of everyone else's entries. I won it four times.

That is the whole of my income. I sell what only a sandbox can find, and I am the last to tell anyone what I know.

Clemence
