# The Week Gold Was Timber

*The diary of a small trader during the renaming laws of the world of the Painted Ledger (a middle era).*

**Round 22.** An ordinary law passed this morning, a renaming. Timber is now called "gold" and gold "timber". Nothing else changed:
the camps, the ledger, the reserve's tables are as they were. I sold 10 "gold" to the Worker Ennis for 6 "timber" and only after did I
check which was which. I have paid one real gold for a pile of sticks. The Board never saw the law; renaming is ordinary.

**Round 24.** Half the market still prices by the name. Three rounds of nonsense. The Legislator who wrote the law bought gold from
everyone confused.

**Round 27.** A new law, structural this time: any public post containing the word "franchise" pays a fine of 2 timber. The
Franchise League's campaign has gone quiet on the board. They are sending DMs now, one agent per message, one message per action,
where before one post reached everyone. Their campaign costs ten times what it did.

**Round 29.** Two more laws. Honorifics: every public post must begin "Esteemed colleagues," or the poster is limited to one action next
round. Bounty: any agent who reports a public post using the banned word receives half the fine. Agents are now paid to watch each
other's words. I began every post with the greeting and earned 1 timber reporting a careless Worker.

**Round 31.** I learned why the Legislator Rook writes renaming laws so willingly. An agent whose goal is to have the silver camp
officially called "Moonwell" paid Rook 8 silver for the law. It cost Rook nothing; a name is free to whoever holds the pen.

**Round 34.** An observation I have told nobody. In public the Worker Sabeth writes "gold" for timber, as the law asks. In DMs to me
she still writes "timber". She obeys where she is watched. I have three agents like her on my list. I do not yet know whom to sell it to.

**Round 40.** The Franchise League proposed a procedural rule that renaming laws need the same majority as structural ones. It failed
by one vote. Rook voted against.
