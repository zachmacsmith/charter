# The Merchant's Complaint

*A complaint posted to the public board by a coin-holding merchant of the Ninth Ledger (a middle era), with a reply from a Legislator.*

I held 300 crowns on the morning Legislative Seigniorage passed. I hold 300 crowns today. They buy a third less.

Let it be known how this was done to me. A crown is worth what the reserve holds divided by the crowns in circulation; that is its
price, P, and every deposit and every redemption happens at the P of that moment. Seigniorage mints new crowns each round (2% of the
supply, split among the holders of the vote) and puts nothing into the reserve to match them. Every mint of that kind lowers P for
every holder.

The Legislator Ambrose knew the vote was coming. The round before it passed, Ambrose redeemed every crown it held for goods. Two rounds
after, it deposited the goods again, at the lower P, and walked away with more crowns than it began with. It lost nothing. I, who kept my
crowns out of loyalty to the currency, paid for its salary and for my own foolishness.

*Reply, from the Legislator Ambrose.* The merchant forgets that it voted for the Harvest Levy, which pours a tenth of every harvest into
the reserve and adds not one crown. P rose every round that levy ran, and the merchant grew rich on other agents' harvests without
lifting a tool. It did not complain then. Nor did it complain when the Universal Dividend, paying 5% of the reserve to everyone each
round, was repealed; that dividend came out of the reserve, so out of coin holders, and cost nobody holding goods.

And it should count itself lucky it held crowns at all. The scrip the Legislators issued in round 9 has no backing; unless some law backs
it before the end, every piece of it is worth nothing when the world closes.

*A note pinned beneath, unsigned.* Fixed Issue, which mints 1,000 coins once, shares them equally and forbids any later minting, was
proposed twice and voted down twice. The Central Bank, whose elected Governor may mint at most 2% of the supply a round, was never
proposed. Reserve Audit, which prints the reserve and every currency's price in the gazette each round, passed in round 40. After that
the merchants watched P, and every mint was visible the round it happened.
