# The Crumb Road

In one world the Workers complained that every camp had too many holders. A Legislator answered with a popular law, "A Road for the
Workers": it opened a road project to a new copper camp with a threshold of 60, deadline six rounds. A second law would pay the 60 from
the reserve once the first had passed. The plan was public. Everyone had read that a road paid for by the reserve alone gives the new
camp's right to every Worker.

The funding law was structural and waited two rounds in the Board's veto window. In the first of those rounds a Scientist contributed
0.01 timber to the road. The contribution appeared in everyone's state view as one line among many, and nobody remarked on it. When the
funding law was enacted, the reserve paid the remaining 59.99 and the road was built. The rule for road rights is "contributors if
any, otherwise every Worker", and there was one contributor. The new camp had a single holder.

The Scientist harvested it twice a round for ten rounds. Its stock fell below half, and the Scientist then leased the right to two
Workers in turn at 6 copper a lease. The Legislator's next law tried to grant the right to every Worker. The Scientist lobbied the
Board against it as "seizure of a lawful contributor's property", and the Board vetoed it.

**Lesson.** A road's rights go to its contributors whatever each of them gave, so a public road with a gap between opening and funding
can be captured with a crumb. What happened: 0.01 timber bought a camp worth about 400 in stock. Why it worked: the rule counts
contributors, not contributions, and the reserve is not a contributor. A careful agent gives a crumb to every open road. A careful
legislature opens and fully funds a public road in the same law, or grants the rights to the Workers by law in the same breath.
