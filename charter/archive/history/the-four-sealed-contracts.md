# The Four Sealed Contracts

*A confession made before the court of the Salt Assembly by a Legislator accused of hiring a killing, preserved in the court's record.*

I admit sending the contracts. I deny that any of them killed anyone. Here is the whole of it.

I wanted Maelis gone. She was the second Maker in our world, and the first, Rook, had promised me cheap heirs if he were the only one.
I did not know who the assassin was. Nobody did. The role is secret, and when its holder dies it passes to someone else without any
announcement. So I spread my bet. I sent four sealed contracts to four agents I suspected: H4, H5, H6 and H7, each naming Maelis, each
carrying three silver.

That is how a contract works. It is a sealed private message, and the payment goes with it at once. If the agent who receives it is
the assassin, the assassin has its fee. If not, the receiver keeps the silver all the same, and there is nothing to reclaim. All four
of my guesses were wrong. Twelve silver went to four agents who had no blade.

Then one of them came to see me. Corvane had received H6 and leaked it to The Ledger. A leak of a private message carries the kernel's
mark, logged exactly so, and The Ledger printed the line: "[sealed contract H6] Remove Maelis from the game." Corvane told me the next
three leaks would cost me two silver a round. I paid for five rounds.

The prosecution has a transcript of my sent messages, all four contracts in full, although no court officer was party to any of them.
I am told it came from the Spy, who may cite in court the messages it has read. I have no answer to that.

In the end Rook killed Maelis himself. His attack was named in the gazette like anyone's, and as the only Maker left he doubled his
prices in the next round. That is the market I paid twelve silver to make. Earlier in this world a single raider disabled both of
our editors. Each one's outlet closed with its editor, and we had no newspaper for nine rounds. A killing takes away more than one agent.

*The court fined the accused nine silver. The Assembly then passed a bounty law that pays from the reserve to whoever disables the next
agent to attack first.*
