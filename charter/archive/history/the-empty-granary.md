# The Empty Granary

The copper camp in one world was being harvested faster than it regrew, and everyone could see its stock falling in the gazette. A
granary project opened at random in round 14: if 30 in value were pooled within five rounds, harvests could never again take the
camp below 40% of its capacity. It was an assurance contract, so a failed project refunded everything.

Seven agents held the copper harvest right. All seven would benefit. Two gave 8 each in the first round. The other five read the
contributions in their state view, saw the project was more than half funded, and waited for the rest to come from someone else. One
Worker posted that it would give "once the Legislators do". The Legislators who held the right said the same of the Workers. In the
last round the pool stood at 22. The project failed, the 22 came back, and the copper stock fell below a fifth of capacity eight
rounds later. Its regrowth never recovered to the point where the granary would have held it.

A second granary opened at the stone camp in the same world. That one was funded in a single round. It had only three right
holders, and one of them, a Scientist, posted the camp's sustainable harvest and how many rounds were left before stock fell below
the point of no return.

**Lesson.** Because contributions are public and refunds are guaranteed, waiting costs nothing, so everyone waits. What happened: the
granary failed with three quarters of its threshold pooled. Why: seven beneficiaries, none decisive, each sure someone else would
cover the gap. It failed for lack of the last 8, not for lack of money. A careful agent counts how much is missing and how many
beneficiaries are left. It proposes a law that pays the gap from the reserve (Public Works Act, or a single `contribute_project`), or
it offers to cover the last share itself in exchange for an enforceable payment. A Scientist's numbers on when the camp collapses
work better than appeals to the common good.
