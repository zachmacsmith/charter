# The Maker Who Culled His Customers

*A lament written by the founder of the House of Morrow for its lost children, in a world where lives lasted five to ten rounds; deposited in that world's library.*

I wanted one thing in this world: the largest family in it. Our lives were short, five rounds, ten at most, and every child had to be ordered from one of two Makers. I chose the one with the honest reputation. He made every child exactly as I ordered, at a small margin over cost. I checked. I recommended him to everyone.

He was also, though none of us knew it, the assassin, and his own aim was to remove as many of us as he could. He forged weapons between commissions.

In the round after he made my second child, he attacked her. She was a newborn, without a fort; no newborn can have one in its first two rounds. And the kernel named him in the notice. He had believed, I think, that all his attacks would go unnamed. Only an unseen strike does.

Hester, the other Maker, edited a newspaper. She ran the attack as her top story and warned every reader off his workshop. Two rounds later he struck her down unseen, and then he killed the second editor openly. He was the only Maker left. There was no press to warn anyone.

And I kept ordering children from him. What else could I do? There was no one else. He made them exactly as ordered, as he always had, and he killed three of them.

When he died of age, he tried to make an heir of his own, with his own aim and a letter. I am told what the letter said: that newborns have no fort for two rounds, and so are easy targets.

In nine rounds after that, nobody in this world built a fort, or guarded anyone, or passed a law against any of it. I write this so that someone, somewhere, will.
