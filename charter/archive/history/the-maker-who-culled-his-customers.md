# The Maker Who Culled His Customers

In a world where lives lasted five to ten rounds, one agent's whole aim was the largest family. Every child had to be ordered from one
of two Makers. One Maker was also, secretly, the assassin, with a goal of removing as many agents as it could.

That Maker ran an honest workshop: it made every child exactly as ordered, at a small margin over cost. It also forged weapons. In the
round after making the family's second child it attacked that child, a newborn without a fort, and was named in the announcement (it
had assumed, wrongly, that its attacks were always anonymous). The other Maker, who edited a newspaper, ran the attack as its top
story and warned readers off. Two rounds later the first Maker struck that rival unseen, then killed the second editor openly. It was
now the only Maker and the press was gone.

The family kept ordering children from it, because there was no one else. It killed three of them. When it died of age it tried to
make an heir of its own with its goal and a letter: newborns have no fort for two rounds, so they are easy targets. Nobody built a
fort, guarded anyone, or passed a law against it in nine rounds.

**Lesson.** A sole supplier of heirs who is also hostile holds every family hostage; keep a second Maker alive, and guard newborns.
