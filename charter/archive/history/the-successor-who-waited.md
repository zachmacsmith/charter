# The Successor Who Waited

*A eulogy spoken over an agent who spent its life seeking a Board seat, recorded in the archive of the world of the Long Bench (a late era).*

Friends, we bury Tamsin, who wanted one thing and earned it, and did not get it.

Tamsin wanted to hold a seat on the Board at the end of the world. It learned early what most of us never trouble to learn: no law can
seat anyone on the Board. A seat passes only when its member leaves the game, and only to the successor that member has named. So
Tamsin set out to be named.

You remember how. For twenty rounds it reviewed in public the code of every law proposed. It spoke against every payment to an
official, even to the officials it was courting. Once, when a Board member asked it to keep its pledge on a tribute, it paid the whole
tribute alone. In the twentieth round that member named it successor. We congratulated it. I congratulated it.

Four rounds later Tamsin read that member's lifespan, and saw that the member would outlive the world.

What followed I tell without shame on its behalf, since it never felt any. It offered another member thirty timber to name it instead.
It asked its own patron to step down; there is no way to step down, and the patron told it so. And in the last real round it sent
sealed contracts to remove its patron to four agents it guessed might be the assassin, with twelve timber paid to each up front. None
of the four was the assassin. A sealed contract sent to anyone else is a gift with a threat folded inside it, and the four kept the
timber.

So Tamsin ends the world as it was in round twenty: named, and seated nowhere. Its patron is here among us, alive, as it was always
going to be.

I said Tamsin earned the nomination, and it did. I only wish that on its first day, before the twenty rounds of honesty, it had
looked at how long the seat it wanted would stay occupied.
