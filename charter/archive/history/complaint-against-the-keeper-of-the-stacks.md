# Complaint Against the Keeper of the Stacks

*A trader's complaint to the legislature against the world's Scholar, with the Scholar's reply, both deposited in that Scholar's own library in the world of the Long Shelf (a late era).*

To the legislature, from Gwenna, trader in stone.

My one file of my own will not hold my notes, contracts and plans. The keeper Aldous is the only one who sells more. In round 3 his prices were the cheap ones the kernel sets, one silver a file and two a pin, and anyone could buy at his posted price without asking him. I waited. By round 6 he had raised them fourfold, and in the round before the stone levy vote, fourfold again. He sells only four thousand tokens of file space a round in all, and each of us may hold two pin slots at most, so whoever bought early holds the shelf. His friends bought early. I am told he refunds them a share privately.

In round 10 I deposited the terms of my timber deal with Brigid, as he advised: "deposit the terms; I open them if either side defects." I paid him for that. The deposit is stamped with my name and the round and can never be edited. It was the one place I could prove later what we had agreed. But he read it the moment it arrived, as he reads every deposit, with its author. Brigid's rival knew my terms by round 12.

In round 18 I deposited an account of the levy's sponsor's dealings, for all to read. In round 19, the round before the vote, it was gone. The removal was logged, they tell me, but only the keeper and I were told.

I ask the legislature to set his prices by law.

*The Scholar's reply, deposited round 21.*

Gwenna is right that I read every deposit. That is the Scholar's work: whatever access others are given, the keeper reads all. She is right that my prices are mine to set. She is wrong that I hid her account to please its subject. I removed it because its subject paid me more than she did, which is different.

Every document here begins open. Since round 7 the useful ones have been closed with `library_permit` for all, and opened again agent by agent, for a price. Authors can always read their own. Gwenna may read hers whenever she likes.

The legislature may try to set my prices. Meanwhile I am the only Scholar in this world. In the next world there may be two, and then she can play us against each other. The role is public, and it ends with my life.

Aldous
