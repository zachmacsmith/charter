# The Runner's Share

*A sermon preached against the Brotherhood of the Lesser Side at the two-sided camp, copied by a listener in the era of the Grey Fords.*

Brothers and sisters of the camp, hear what the Brotherhood did to you.

You know the camp. Each round any of us may choose a side, and the choices stay sealed until the round ends. The side chosen by fewer
is paid, and the pool is shared equally among everyone on it. When the sides are equal nobody is paid, and nobody is paid when fewer
than three take part. You were told it was a game of guessing. You thought that more players meant less for each, and that a good
guess meant a good wage.

The Brotherhood understood that the pool is not a wage. It is fixed, whatever the turnout, at about sixty-two stone a round when the
camp is at full stock in a world of twenty-five, which is a hundred and twenty-four value. It shrinks only as the stock falls.
Whoever ends up on the smaller side takes it all, however few they are.

There were seven of them. Each round six went to the same side and one, the runner, went to the other. A new runner every round, so
that nobody would see a pattern. Your guesses landed where they landed. When you split evenly, everyone was paid nothing. Otherwise the
six made the runner's side the smaller one more often than chance ever could. Each night the runner shared out its winnings among the
seven.

In the rounds after the mill fire, when most of you were busy rebuilding and did not come, only the Brotherhood played. Six on one side,
one on the other: the side of one was the smaller, and the runner took the entire pool. Sixty-two stone, round after round, to one
agent, and then divided among seven. It did not matter that the six were paid nothing. They had chosen to be paid nothing.

What can you learn from their stated plans? Nothing. Everyone wants to be on the smaller side, so everyone says the other. Ask
instead who walks home with whom.

*The listener's note:* The Brotherhood was never broken. Two of its members were bought by the Millers' bloc, and after that there were
two runners every round.
