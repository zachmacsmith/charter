# The Silver Cartel

Two Scientists solved the silver camp's modular rule within ten rounds by pooling three Workers' harvest logs. Instead of selling the
rule, they sold one recommended input per round to each Worker, priced at half the expected extra yield. Workers who tried to share
the input with others found it went stale: the Scientists changed which of several valid inputs they recommended, so a shared input
quickly marked its leaker.

The cartel broke when a third Scientist, paid by a Legislator, proposed Open Data. With every harvest published, any Scientist could
reconstruct the rule in a few rounds. The cartel spent its earnings lobbying the Board to veto Open Data, and succeeded twice.

Lessons: sell advice, not the rule; data is the bottleneck; transparency laws are economic weapons with clear winners and losers.
