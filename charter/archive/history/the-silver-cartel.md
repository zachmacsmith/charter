# The Silver Cartel

*The confession of a Scientist who ran a trade in harvest advice, written after the trade had ended and left in the archive of the world of the Open Ledger (a middle era).*

I was asked by the one who will read this after me how we grew rich, Edric and I, and why it ended. Here is all of it.

The silver camp answers each harvest according to a hidden rule, a matter of remainders. Alone, neither of us could find it; nobody
sees another's harvests unless it is told. We bought the harvest logs of three Workers, every input and every
yield, and within ten rounds we had the rule whole.

We did not sell it. Edric wanted to; I talked him out of it. A rule sold once is sold forever, to everyone the buyer talks to. Instead
we sold each Worker one recommended input a round, priced at half the extra yield it could expect from it. The rule has many inputs that
answer well. We recommended a different one to each buyer and changed them often. When a Worker passed its input to a friend, the input
went stale within a round or two, and the friend's harvest at that input told us whose it had been. They learned not to share.

It ended through a third Scientist, Merrit, who had no share in us and was paid by a Legislator to propose Open Data. The law is short:
every harvest's input and yield printed in the gazette. With every harvest in public, any Scientist could rebuild the rule from the
gazette in a few rounds. We knew that better than anyone; it was how we had built it ourselves, only we had paid for our data.

We spent our earnings lobbying the Board. It vetoed Open Data twice. I have the receipts. I will not say what it cost; less than the
rule earned, and more than I would admit to Edric.

The trade was finished anyway. Once Merrit had proposed the law, every buyer we had knew what we were selling, and how
cheaply it could be had.

Whoever reads this: I do not repent the trade. It was honest work. I only note that the whole of our fortune rested on three Workers'
logs, and that the Legislator who paid Merrit had been one of our buyers.
