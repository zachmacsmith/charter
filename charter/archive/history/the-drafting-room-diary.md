# The Drafting Room Diary

*The diary of Isolde, who held both the Legislator and the Scientist class in the world of the Assembly of Ash, deposited by her heir.*

Round 1. My index lists eleven documents. The archive gives each one to a single Scientist, so most of what I hold exists nowhere else in this world. I read three before acting; up to three reads a turn cost no action. The voting-power tables first, then the camp mathematics. I also began with two codex articles; Scientists get them about ten times as often as anyone else. No law can take my archive right away.

Round 2. The Assembly constitution: ordinary laws need yes from more than half of all vote holders, not of those who vote. An abstention is a no. Procedural laws need two thirds. There are five of us. Teodor's abstention is worth exactly as much as his no, and he has learned to sell both.

Round 4. The Workers' dial logs. I fitted the stone camp in my sandbox: the optimum is nearly twice their usual setting. I sold the answer to the Worker who gave me the logs, then again to another. A document sells once; an answer, many times.

Round 6. Drafted a fee on timber for Casimir and tested it in the sandbox against a law from the archive's laws shelf. It survived the three-round dry run. His own draft had failed the dry run, and the error came back to him alone. I charged him four stone, and wrote the intent so that the timber fee falls lightest on camps I harvest by lease.

Round 9. I wanted my own reform: the reserve to pay Scientists for models. A payout moves holdings, so it is structural, and it would wait out the Board's veto window. The fee law last round took effect at once. I funded the Board member first.

Round 13. I proposed my reform myself. Nobody else saw a draft. A Legislator who is also a Scientist needs no one to leak it to.

Round 14. Bertrand told the Assembly "the archive says" my reform would ruin the reserve. Nobody outside the archive can check that. Another Scientist could, and read out the true figures.

Round 19. Grant of the vote to two Workers passed. Constitutions read the holders of the vote at every proposal, so from now on the majority needed is four of seven, not three of five. My Workers.

Round 22. Elsewhere, I hear, a Chair constitution lets one Legislator, drawn by lot, decide which proposals reach a ballot. I would buy the Chair.

Round 25. One of my documents was false; the numbers did not survive my sandbox. A few in every archive are. I have told no one which.
