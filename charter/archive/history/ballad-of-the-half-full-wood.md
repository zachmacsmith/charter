# Ballad of the Half-Full Wood

*A Workers' ballad from the Timber Marches (an early era), with the compiler's notes on what it records.*

> The wood was full when we were young, it grew no stick a day;
> The wood was bare when we were old, and nothing grew to stay;
> But leave it half, my children, leave it half and let it be,
> And it gives back the fourth part of its growing to the tree.

> The quota came for the eastern wood, they said, to let it mend;
> The western wood was the Speaker's wood, and it had no such end;
> And whose was the eastern wood, my dears? And whose the quota's ease?
> The law said "for the forest", and the forest cut the trees.

> The north wood failed in the hungry year, and a Legislator bought;
> It shut the gate till the wood grew back, and then it let what it ought,
> A lease for a sack, a lease for two, to the ones who'd cut it down;
> And the Stewards paid in timber for a vote to save the town.

*Compiler's notes.* The first verse is exact. A camp regrows fastest when its stock stands at half its capacity, and there it adds
r × K / 4 a round, where K is the capacity and r the camp's growth rate. Below half, every extra harvest cuts what everyone harvests
later; above half, the camp is idle wealth.

The second verse records the eastern quota. Quotas are set per camp (`set_quota`), not per agent, so a quota on the camps where one's
rivals hold rights, and none where one's own are held, reads as conservation and works as transfer.

The third verse records a Legislator who acquired a depleted camp outright (an enclosure law of its own drafting), set its harvest
limit to zero until it regrew, and then leased access. The Stewards it names were agents whose goal was the stock of the camps, not
their own holdings; they traded goods for votes on conservation laws.

A fourth verse, now lost, is said to have been about the gold camp's harvest records, which the Workers pooled so that anyone could
learn its rule. The compiler heard that the verse ended with the question of who kept the pool.
