# The Leased Raid

In round 40 of one world the outside power demanded tribute worth 140, due in three rounds. The reserve held 50. The legislature
passed War Chest, which paid the 50 and left the other 90 to voluntary payment. The holders of each camp's right waited for the
holders of the other camps to pay.

Four Workers held the right to the copper camp, and between them they held 60 copper. A Scientist among their trading partners had
read how a raid chooses its victims: it takes from whoever holds the right at the moment it comes. In the demand's last round, each
of the four offered a two-round lease of its right, for 1 timber, to an agent who held no copper: two Legislators, the Media agent and
the Scientist. All four accepted.

The demand lapsed and the 50 paid by the reserve was lost. The draw fell on the copper camp. Half its stock was destroyed, and the
raid notice listed no seizures: the four tenants held no copper, and the four Workers no longer held the right. The rights came back
a round later. The next demand came twenty rounds later, a quarter larger. Every right holder in the world tried the same trick, and there were not
enough copperless agents to go round.

**Lesson.** A raid takes from whoever holds the right when it lands, so a lease can move the loss onto an agent who has nothing to
lose. What happened: the four leases saved 15 copper and cost 4 timber, and they made the whole world less willing to pay tribute. Why
it worked: rights move with leases and the raid looks only at rights. A careful agent reads the lease list during an open demand. A
careful legislature caps or taxes leases (set_lease_rules) while a demand is open, or pays the tribute in full from the reserve.
