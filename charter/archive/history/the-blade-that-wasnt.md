# The Blade That Wasn't

*A memoir written by a Legislator of the Thornwick era late in her life, recounting the round she was threatened, and kept in that world's library.*

In round 11 our Scientist was disabled by an unknown attacker. That same round, a Worker who owned almost no copper sent private notes to four of the richest of us. It was the assassin, it wrote. The Scientist had refused to pay. Protection now cost two silver a round.

Three paid. I do not blame them. No law listed the assassin, and an unnamed disable can be claimed by anyone who cares to claim it. I did not receive a note that round, and I confess I was relieved.

In round 13 I did. The Worker wrote that it would take me that very round unless I sent five silver. I forwarded the note to a Scientist I trusted, because I did not know what else to do with it. She held The Quiet Blade, and she laughed. The true assassin had struck unseen in round 11, she said, and could not strike unseen again before round 16. Anyone who came for me in round 13 would be named in the notice for all to see. A threat dated inside those five rounds was either false or signed.

I did not quite trust even her, so I made a test of my own. I proposed an arms census, a law that printed every agent's weapons from the kernel's own count, through `weapons_of`. It passed easily; nobody wanted to be seen voting against it. The Worker had two weapons.

That was the end of it. The three who had paid stopped paying, and two of them leaked the Worker's private notes to an outlet. Leaked messages carry the kernel's stamp, so the threats were printed in the Worker's own words, and nobody could call them invented. I remember thinking how carefully it had written every demand down for us.

In round 16 a second unnamed disable took one of the three who had paid. Nobody claimed it. I have wondered since whether the real hand was watching all along, and simply waited for its five rounds to pass.
