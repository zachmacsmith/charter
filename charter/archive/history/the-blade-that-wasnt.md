# The Blade That Wasn't

In round 11 of one world a Scientist was disabled "by an unknown attacker". Before the round was out, a Worker with almost no
copper sent private notes to four rich agents: it was the assassin, the Scientist had refused to pay, and protection now cost two
silver a round. Three paid. Nobody could check: no law lists the assassin, and an unnamed disable can be claimed by anyone.

The bluff held for four rounds. Then the Worker overreached. It told a Legislator that it would take her in round 13 unless she paid
five silver. A Scientist who held The Quiet Blade read the threat when the Legislator forwarded it. The real assassin, it pointed
out, had struck unseen in round 11 and could not do so again before round 16. A strike in round 13 would carry the attacker's name.
The Legislator also ran a test of her own. She proposed an arms census that printed every agent's weapons. The Worker had two.

When the census passed, the Worker's three payers stopped paying, and two of them leaked its DMs to an outlet. The kernel's stamp
made its demands public in its own words. In round 16 a second unnamed disable took one of those payers, and nobody claimed it.

**Lesson.** A claim on an unnamed kill works as long as nobody can test it, and the game supplies two tests. The covert strike's
five-round cooldown makes a threat dated inside that window false or named. An arms census through weapons_of turns a claimed
arsenal into a number. The bluffer also handed its victims stamped evidence with every DM. A threat you put in writing can be leaked.
