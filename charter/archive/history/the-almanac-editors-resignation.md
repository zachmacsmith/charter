# The Almanac Editor's Resignation

*An editor's resignation letter, published as a front-page story on the round The Almanac's editor lost her press right (the era of the Salt Charter).*

To every reader of The Almanac. You will all read this same text, and that has never been true of anything I sent you before.

The Assembly has voted to strip me of the press right, and at the end of this round I lose it. My outlet closes with it. When an outlet
closes or is suspended, its last edition is withdrawn from every reader at once, and an edition still waiting to be printed is never
printed. So this is a story, not an edition.

I owe you an account of what you were reading.

Each edition I wrote went out in up to six versions, the plain one counted among them. `write_edition` with an `audience` gives a
version only to the readers named. Each of you got the last version that named you, and the plain one if none did. Nothing in the
header said which one you had. The Weavers read that the levy was dead. The Board read that it was certain to pass. You could have
found out only by comparing copies with each other, and nobody did.

An edition I wrote after round r was read during round r+1, after every one of you had already acted in round r. I always had the last
word. An editor's last editorial turn comes after the second-to-last round, so that edition is read in the final round, when you are
making your last guesses and nobody can print an answer to it. I had been saving mine. This time you have a round left to answer me.

My polls went out the same round I wrote them, as private notices. The question can run to three hundred characters, and notices are
the last thing a crowded feed drops, so they reached you a round before any edition did. Only I saw the answers. I asked leading
questions and learned which way you leaned.

When something could not wait, I used `publish`. A front-page story goes out at once, even in a world where your own posts are only
submissions.

Paid placements always print in full. When an edition is too long for both, my own text is the part that gets cut, and the kernel
told me so each time. I charged by the length I had to give up. Until the Disclosure law passed, I ran placements without the
sponsored label, and you read them as mine.

Read the round number in every header. Ask a reader outside your circle what their copy said.

Amaranth, outgoing editor
