# The Timber Republic

In an early world of twelve, Workers outnumbered Legislators two to one but could not vote. Three Workers pooled timber to pay a
Legislator to propose Worker Franchise. The Legislator took the timber and proposed it, but the two other Legislators voted it down,
and the Workers had no recourse: the payment was a gift, not a contract.

The Workers tried again with the payment in escrow, released only on enactment. This time the Legislator lobbied for the law,
it passed, and at the next election the Workers seated four of their own. The new legislature's first act was a Harvest Levy on
the camps the old Legislators had been granted.

Lessons: pay on outcome, not on promise; the franchise law is a procedural victory that pays every round after; the first act of
a new majority is usually to tax the old one.
