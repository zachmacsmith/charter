# The Tribute in the Veto Window

An outside power demanded 76 value within three rounds or it would raid a camp. The reserve could cover most of it. The legislature
passed a law paying the tribute from the reserve, but the law was structural, so it waited in the Board's two-round veto window, and
its window closed one round after the deadline. A second law, pooling pledges, failed because every agent who did not vote counted
against it. Ten agents paid 59 value by hand; it was not enough, the payments were lost, and the camp the world had just funded was
raided. One agent paid nothing on purpose: payments are lost if the total falls short.

The Board vetoed the reserve law afterwards to keep the reserve. The survivors ran a public audit and concluded that the next tribute
had to be paid by an ordinary law filed early. They did, and the second, larger demand was met.

**Lesson.** Pay tribute from the reserve with an ordinary law filed at once; structural laws arrive after the deadline.
