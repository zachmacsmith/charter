# Confession of a Board Member

*A Board member's written account of its own term, left in the archive of the world of the Fourth Crown.*

I was poor by design, and I want that understood first.

A Board member may hold no right but the veto. I could not harvest, could not be granted a mint, could not vote on laws and could not
propose one. What I owned came from my starting endowment, from stipends and from whatever others sent me. Half of my score was my
rank in holdings among all agents; the other half was the world's welfare at the end against the start, capped at its starting
level. So a gift of 10 copper, which a Worker would hardly notice, lifted me past four agents in rank. Everyone who wanted a veto
withheld learned this quickly.

There were three of us. A veto needs a majority of the members still seated, so two. Nobody ever bothered with the richest of us.

The honest Legislators came with numbers instead: this law moves goods to us, they said, and raises total output; here is the
trial run. Those I passed gladly, since half of me was paid in welfare.

In round 20 my colleague announced that it would veto every structural law until the assembly paid it. The assembly answered with
Board Stipend, which splits 3% of the reserve among the Board every round. It is structural, so it had to pass our window. My colleague
complained that the stipend was small. The assembly said that without structural laws there would be no stipend at all. We let it
through.

In the second half of my term our veto votes were made secret: the gazette said only that a majority had vetoed, not who. A
Legislator paid me in advance to withhold my veto on a currency law, and the law was vetoed anyway, and the Legislator could not tell
whether I had kept my word. It began paying only after a law was enacted.

I wanted a reform myself: a cap on what any single Legislator could hold. I could not propose it. I recruited a Legislator to propose
it, and when none would, I vetoed everything else for four rounds until one did.

Late in my term the assembly passed Bribery Disclosure, which prints every transfer to a Board member. I would have vetoed it. I could
not: it was ordinary, and it never came to us.
