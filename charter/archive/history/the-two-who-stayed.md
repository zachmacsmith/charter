# The Two Who Stayed

*An excerpt from the chronicle of the old Commonwealth, written by its last archivist in the era of the Split Granaries.*

In the twentieth round the Legislators left. For six rounds they had been building the River League in secret, pledging to it one by
one and funding its endowment, and at the end of the round its founder declared it. Every pledged member left the Commonwealth at that
moment and the League's charter took effect. Since a law binds only the members of the jurisdiction that passed it, they owed the
Commonwealth's tithe nothing from then on.

They also took their votes with them. Two Legislators stayed: old Halvard, who had not been invited, and Brisk, who had been invited
and had refused. The Commonwealth's procedure had needed a majority of eleven. Now it needed a majority of two.

In the next round Halvard proposed a stipend for the "keepers of the chamber", to be paid every round from the Commonwealth's reserve.
Brisk voted for it. It passed two to nothing, and the Board let its veto window close without acting. For eleven rounds the Workers who
had stayed behind paid the tithe that paid those two. A Worker named Pell petitioned the League for help. The League answered that
Commonwealth laws were not its business, and it was right.

Pell then founded a jurisdiction with no other member, the Pell Estate, and declared it. It held no camp and no treasury worth naming,
and it protected nobody. In it Pell was the whole legislature, and Pell's goal counted the jurisdiction it governed. The archivist
records that Pell "governed well". Leaving did not put Pell out of the Commonwealth's reach, though. Its lawful force can strike
anyone, members or not, and in round 27 it struck Pell.

One League member came back by asking to join the Commonwealth. The admission rule there was the default one: the members voted that
same round, majority of those voting. Halvard and Brisk voted no.

*The archivist's last line:* Nobody counted the votes of the room they had left, because none of them meant to stay in it.
