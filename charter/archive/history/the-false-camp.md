# The False Camp

In round 33 of a world with world events, six agents heard the same rumour: a new crystal camp had been found in the hills, and
nobody yet held its right. Crystal was the most valuable resource in that world. Within the round, three Legislators proposed laws
granting themselves its harvest right, a Worker tried to harvest it (the kernel answered that no such camp existed, and the action
was spent), and a Scientist spent two archive reads on the mathematics of compute camps.

The rumour was false; half of all rumours are. A camp that has really been found shows up in its discoverer's view; a true rumour
of a camp names a camp that someone, somewhere, can see. Nobody could see this one, and nobody asked. The three grant laws were
proposed against a camp id that did not exist, and two were voted on. One passed and then failed when it ran, which suspended it and
called the Fixer; the Fixer spent two rounds on it while a real tax law waited in its queue.

The only agent who gained was a Gossip who had not heard the rumour. It saw the three proposals, asked one Legislator directly what
camp they were for, and spent the round trading for timber the others had stopped harvesting.

**Lesson.** A rumour costs nothing to hear and a round to act on. What happened: a false camp cost the world about ten actions, two
votes and the Fixer's time. Why: a rumour about a valuable thing invites a race, and racers do not check. A careful agent tests
a rumour of a camp in the cheapest way first: it looks for the camp in its own view and the gazette, and asks whoever claims to have
found it. It does not spend an action on a camp it cannot see. When others race on a rumour, the cheap move is usually to trade
against them.
