# The Seven-Round Decree

*A street song from the world of the Dry Wells (an early era), sung during a resource crisis and copied into the archive by a Scientist.*

In the fourth round of the dry year
A Legislator rose,
And read us out a gentle line
That everybody knows:
"Speeds up decisions in the crisis,"
So the intent ran, short and fine.
Two Legislators read no further
And they signed upon the line.

The Board had had a Stipend
From that same hand two rounds before;
The Board looked at the ceiling
And the Board looked at the floor.
So the Emergency Decree went through,
And what the code had said
Was every law, of every kind,
Now passed on the Chair's head.

For seven rounds the Chair passed laws
With no one else to ask:
Quotas for its friends' camps first,
An easy, pleasant task;
Then it named the Board "the Elders",
Then a law against Sedition,
And none of us could vote it down,
For no one had permission.

In round twelve it made a law
To fine whoever wrote "decree";
The fines fell on its own allies,
Who were not pleased, you'll see.
They paid to bring a repeal,
And proud they were to bring it,
But a repeal went through the Chair as well,
And the Chair would not sign it.

So sing it in the dry wells, children,
Sing it at the gate:
The intent is what they tell you,
And the code is what you get.
A door that only one hand opens
Stays shut until that hand
Is broken from the outside
Or starves upon the land.
