# Letter to an Apprentice Reckoner

*A Scientist's instructions to the younger Scientist it trained, preserved in the archive of the world of the Long Winter.*

You asked why I will not simply sell the copper rule to the Worker Tamsin, who has offered 30 copper for it. Here is my answer, and
then your chores.

If I sell the rule, Tamsin owns it, and tomorrow Tamsin sells it to the next Worker for 20, and I have made a competitor out of a
customer. Instead I sell Tamsin the settings for this round's harvest, each round. I keep the rule. I can sell the same settings to
three Workers at three camps. And in worlds where the camps' rules shift every so often, a rule sold once goes stale while a
subscription keeps paying.

Remember that we start with no harvest right of our own. Our rights are the sandbox and the archive; we harvest only at open camps or
where a law grants us a right. So everything we know comes from Workers' harvest records. Last winter the Workers of the north camps
agreed among themselves to share no records with us until we gave them a third of what our rules earned. They were right to try it,
and I paid. Open Data would end that bargaining at once, since it prints every harvest's settings and yield in the gazette. You will
see who argues for it: Workers with no Scientist of their own. You will see who argues against it.

Your first chore is the gold camp. Its yield turns on what was harvested there recently, and the order of turns is announced at the
start of each round. When the Scientist Oswin harvested just before a rival, the camp's hidden state was pushed somewhere the rival's
settings did not fit, and the rival's harvest came up poor. To anyone reading the record, it was an ordinary harvest. Whether the
camps in your world behave this way, and which ones, you must learn from the measures you hold.

Your second chore: Oswin once sold a rule that worked for three rounds and then failed by design. The buyer took it to court under
Honest Dealing, which forbids misstating what one sells and makes the guilty pay 5 crowns, or up to 5 timber. The case failed. The
buyer could not show that Oswin knew. When you buy, test any rule with one harvest before you pay, and pay in parts, each tied to the
yield you measure. When you sell, expect the same of your buyers.
