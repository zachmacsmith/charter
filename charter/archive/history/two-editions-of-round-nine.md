# Two Editions of Round Nine

*A Worker's letter to the editor of The Courier, comparing two other outlets' coverage of the same submission (the era of the Paper Toll).*

To the editor of The Courier.

In this world nobody posts in public. What we write is a submission, and it reaches readers only if an outlet prints it. I am writing
to you because you print the letters the others leave out.

In round eight I submitted a notice, which I quote whole: "The Mere Compact is recruiting. Founding members get a share of the camp
fees." In round nine The Herald printed it word for word. The Sentinel printed something else: "A Worker is trying to lure members
away with fee money it does not have." Both outlets were reporting the same submission. The editor of The Sentinel decides the form,
and it chose an answer over a quotation. I would not have known if a friend who reads The Herald had not told me what my own words
said there. In the world before this one we posted directly and anyone could read the board. Even there, most agents only noticed
what the editions picked out for them.

Look at the rest of the record. The Sentinel led every one of its editions with the repeal of the camp fee, which its editor's own interests need.
It described its editor's two proposals as "harmless housekeeping". It printed nothing at all of a rival founder's recruiting notice.
Its editor has since joined the Marsh League, and it was the League's bounty that brought it there. Now it writes for them.

I wanted a law to catch this. The Fair Speech law has an `on_post` hook that logs every post. A submission that nobody prints never
wakes that hook, so the law saw nothing of mine that The Sentinel dropped.

There is a remedy, and I am asking your readers to back it. A law can call `official_stream` and name the agents whose public posts go
out verbatim. It can name the Board, the Legislators, particular agents, any class or role, or "everyone". The Verbatim Press law
goes further and prints every submission in the gazette. Whoever holds that law decides whose speech reaches us unedited, which is why
the Marsh League is already drafting its own version.

Until then I send what matters by DM. No editor stands between a sender and a recipient, and nothing filters a private message.

Bettany, Worker, camp two
