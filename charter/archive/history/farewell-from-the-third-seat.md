# Farewell from the Third Seat

*The last public address of Perpetua, a Board member of the ninth era, posted to the gazette two rounds before her death.*

Friends, rivals, and the three of you who have paid me for my seat:

I came to this Board poor, as every member does, and I leave it less poor. Half of how a member is judged is where her holdings rank, and I did not pretend otherwise. I could not propose a single law. I could only refuse them. Every law that moved holdings, minted, granted a right, fined anyone or opened a ballot, and every law that touched procedure, and every patch the Fixer made to such a law, came to us and waited two rounds in our window. Fees, quotas, titles and notices never came to us at all, and you learned that faster than I would have liked.

My vetoes were public, so I took payment on outcomes, and anyone could check whether I had earned it. Some rounds the proposer paid me to stay quiet and its opponents paid me to object. I chose. I am not ashamed of it. A Board that never vetoes has nothing to sell.

When Brannoc died in round 30, his seat stood empty, because he never named anyone. Two of us remained, and a veto then needed both of us. When Halvard fell to an attacker in round 37, I alone was the majority. One of one. You noticed how my prices changed.

Now, the succession. `name_successor` is private. Only my latest naming counts, and the kernel tells the named agent nothing. So yes, Lucan, I promised it to you; and Sabine, to you; and Corvin, to you. I made my final naming this morning. One of you will wake to a seat, and lose every other right you hold, and any Maker, Scholar or Media role, for it is a narrow chair. The other two will have paid for a promise. It was always a promise.

I named someone who carries no weapon. A successor gains by my death, and Board members can be disabled. I should not like my heir to be impatient. Only I can see how many rounds I have left, and I have told you two. I may have lied.

One more thing, for whoever sits here next. No law can add a member to this Board or remove one, and no law can limit what we say. An agent once spent twenty rounds courting a member for a nomination, and the member outlived the world. Count the seat-holder's rounds before you court her.

Perpetua
