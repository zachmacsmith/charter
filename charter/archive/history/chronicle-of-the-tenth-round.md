# Chronicle of the Tenth Round

*An excerpt from the chronicle of the Harrow Assembly (a middle era), covering its first two elections.*

In the ninth round the old Legislators, knowing their seats would be contested, passed Worker Franchise. It created the right
`elector`, gave it to every Worker then living, and called a ballot every tenth round to seat five Legislators by approval: each
elector approves as many candidates as it likes, the ballot stays open two rounds, and the five most approved take the vote and the
right to propose, while every sitting Legislator loses them. In the same ninth round, the outgoing Legislators passed a second law
to bind whoever came after them: once the round-10 election was over, it repealed the franchise and put in its place a copy that held
the next election in round 40 instead of round 20. The chronicle does not record that the Workers noticed.

There were twelve electors. Five of them, the quarry crews, met in private and agreed on five names, and every one of the five approved
exactly those five and no others. The other seven scattered their approvals over eleven candidates. When the ballot closed, four of the quarry's five names were seated. The seven had more votes and fewer seats.

The new chamber's first quarrel was about Renunciation, which lets any Legislator give up the vote, irreversibly, for a harvest right
at a camp of its choosing. The quarry faction offered two rivals rights at the silver camp. Both renounced. Three Legislators remained,
and the quarry held all three.

Elsewhere in the same era, the chronicler notes, a world drew its Legislators by Sortition, five at random every tenth round from
everyone but the Board and the Fixer. Nobody could buy a seat beforehand. But the draw was public the moment it was made, and buying
three of five newly drawn Legislators cost less than one campaign.

And in a third world, under Wealth-Weighted Vote, each Legislator's ballot weighed as much as its holdings. The weights were fixed at
the moment a proposal opened its ballot. One Legislator borrowed heavily the round before it proposed a law, carried the vote on
borrowed weight, and repaid the next round.

In a fourth, the electorate was tied to the land: a law gave `elector` only to holders of harvest rights, and Licence Auction sold
the harvest rights every ten rounds, two to each camp's highest bidders, payable in timber. The chronicler calls this the honest
market in votes. Only the last of the four worlds had written down who counts as a voter in a procedural law, harder to change
than the franchise itself; its electorate was the only one that lasted.
