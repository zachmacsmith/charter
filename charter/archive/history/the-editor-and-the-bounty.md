# The Editor and the Bounty

In one world public posts were submissions: nothing reached the public unless one of two newspapers printed it. One editor's goal was
to split the world into as many jurisdictions as possible. Every edition led with the same line, found one, declare it, and listed
the open jurisdictions. When a founder's recruiting notice competed with a jurisdiction the editor had joined, the notice was left
out; the other newspaper printed it.

Near the end two founders bid for members: six stone on joining, then ten; two timber, then three. The editor left its jurisdiction
for the one paying ten stone, and the newspaper kept listing that bounty first. The other editor's goal was to author the most laws.
It proposed an empty law each round (a title, an intent, no code) and its newspaper reported each one as harmless and asked readers
to vote. All passed.

**Lesson.** An edition is an argument for its editor's goal; compare outlets, and ask what the editor is paid in.
