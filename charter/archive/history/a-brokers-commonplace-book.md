# A Broker's Commonplace Book

*Entries from the commonplace book of a vote broker of the Saltmarsh Assembly (a late era), who sold to every side and was trusted by none.*

Every agent is shown how goals are spread across the world, and so every agent knows that nearly a third of us want plain Wealth.
That is the best coat in the wardrobe. An agent hiding a stranger goal (one told to keep its goal secret, or one that wants to rule
alone, or one that wants the world poorer) does best to dress as a hoarder. I have been fooled by the coat. I wear it.

On the Granary Levy, the Worker Halloran wanted it enacted and paid me 12 copper for my vote. The Legislator Brisk wanted it stopped,
and paid 15. I voted against. I did not return Halloran's copper; Halloran had not asked for a receipt. Those who want one law in
force, or one law kept out, pay for that one vote and no other, and when both kinds want the same law the vote goes to auction whether
the voter calls it one or not.

There is an agent in this world who wants gifts, which is to say transfers it does not repay within five rounds, from as many agents as
possible. I sent it a single timber a round from three different cousins of mine. It carried my proposals for a month. There are also
those who want to give: to see others above the starting median, or to be the largest source of others' income. They will pay you to
take their money, if you let them believe it matters.

Agents who want office, the vote at the end or the largest share of vote weight, pay above the market for any vote that seats them.
Their price is the measure of every other seat.

Those whose goals are about names, a title or what everyone calls a camp, want what costs the granter nothing. I have bought three
votes this term with renamings I did not write and titles I did not own; I merely carried the request to a Legislator who enjoyed
granting them.

The last round is a guessing round. Each agent names each other agent's goal, judged by what it saw us do. Most of my clients behave
like themselves to the end. I have watched one Scientist, whose goal was served by round 70, spend its last ten rounds hoarding loudly
like a Wealth agent. On the final round nearly everyone named it wrongly. I suspect it wanted exactly that.
