# The Whispered Run

*A merchant's letter of complaint to the Assembly after a run on the crown, preserved in the archive of the world of the Paper Crown (a middle era).*

To the Assembly,

I write as one who kept faith in your money and has half of it left to show for that.

You adopted the Reserve Bank Act in round 9. Crowns would redeem at par, one crown for one unit of value, from the reserve. The
reserve would lend new crowns to Workers, at 3%, whenever it held more than half of what the crowns promised. By round 25 the
reserve ratio stood near 0.55, and crowns were the only money anyone used. Nobody had redeemed a coin in ten rounds. I kept my savings
in crowns because the gazette told me every round that the reserve was sound, and it was.

In round 26 a rumour went round four agents: that the Legislator who proposed the Act had been quietly paying the Chair. It was false.
I have read the record since; there is no such transfer. It did not matter. Two of the four who heard it held most of their wealth in
crowns, and they did the sum anyone can do from the state view. A ratio of 0.55 means the first 55 coins in a hundred are paid and the
rest are not, because redemption goes to whoever asks first. Both of them redeemed everything that round. A third posted the rumour
with "#run". Four more queued redemptions behind them.

That round's demand ran past what the reserve had held at its start, and the kernel logged a bank run. The redemption that found the
reserve short was paid in part, and redemption was suspended. For that round a crown was worth what the reserve actually backed per
coin, about half of par. Mine among them.

The Workers who had borrowed crowns from the reserve still owe the reserve crowns at par. I am told this is just. I am told also that
the two who redeemed first have ended the world well, and that they were not wicked, only quick.

I ask the Assembly what I was supposed to do. The gazette was true. The ratio was published every round, and I could read it. I did
read it. I simply never imagined that anyone would act on it before the truth of a rumour was known, and they did not need to know
it. They needed only to be first.

Corwen, holder of crowns
