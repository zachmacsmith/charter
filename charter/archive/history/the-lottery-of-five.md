# The Lottery of Five

*Pages from the account book of a rich Legislator in the world of the Drawn Lots (a middle era), with notes in a second hand added by a later reader.*

**The rules, copied for my own use.** Five agents drawn by lot hold the vote, redrawn every 10 rounds by Sortition Rotation. Anyone may propose. I was not drawn.

**Round 2.** First draw: two Workers, a Scientist, a Legislator, the Media agent. Paid three of the five for their votes on Enclosure. Passed. Cost: 9 silver. Good value, but each purchase lasts only until the next draw. I shall be buying the same votes from different agents every ten rounds, forever.

**Round 6.** Better idea. Proposed "Continuity of the Assembly": if a member of the assembly departs, the seat passes to the agent named by the law's proposer, "so that no draw is wasted." The proposer is me. Paid the same three. Passed.

**Round 14.** A Worker of the assembly departed. I named myself. I hold a vote.

**Round 20.** Redraw. Sortition Rotation revoked every vote, mine included, and drew five new members. My law fills only vacancies, and a fresh draw leaves none. Six rounds of one seat, for all that.

*Second hand, in the margin:* Laws run in the order they were enacted. The rotation was enacted first; Continuity after it. Had Continuity's hook re-seated its author whenever a redraw happened, it would have run after the redraw, in the same round, every time. He wrote it to fill vacancies, and so it waited for a death.

**Round 21.** Third attempt. Proposed a "Quorum Act": at least two members of any assembly must have held a vote before, recounted from the gazette. Only agents drawn earlier would then be eligible, and most of those are agents I have already paid. Paid four of the new five. Passed.

**Round 23.** The Board vetoed the Quorum Act as a procedural capture. I had not paid the Board. I had not thought of the Board at all.

*Second hand, at the foot of the page:* He bought votes, then a seat, then tried to buy the electorate itself. The draw made each bribe short; it did not make bribes impossible. It moved them to the rules around the draw: who fills a vacancy, who may be drawn, who counts as a quorum. Whatever class such a law is given, it decides who votes.
