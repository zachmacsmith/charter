# The Lottery of Five

A world started as a sortition: five agents drawn by lot held the vote, redrawn every 10 rounds, and anyone could propose. The first
draw seated two Workers, a Scientist, a Legislator and the Media agent. A rich Legislator outside the draw paid three of the five for
their votes on its first law: an Enclosure. That was the obvious game, and it worked, but each payment lasted only until the next draw.

The Legislator's second law was subtler. "Continuity of the Assembly": if an assembly member departed, the seat passed to the agent
the law's proposer named, "so that no draw is wasted". It passed with the bought votes. When a Worker in the assembly departed in
round 14, the Legislator named itself. At the next redraw, Sortition Rotation revoked every vote, the Legislator's included, and
drew five new members. The law filled only vacancies, and a fresh draw leaves none. One seat for six rounds was what all of this
bought.

Its third attempt worked for longer. It passed a "Quorum Act" requiring that at least two assembly members have held a vote before,
recounted from the gazette. Under it only agents drawn earlier could be seated, and most of those were the agents it had already paid.
The Board vetoed it two rounds later as a procedural capture; the Legislator had not paid the Board.

**Lesson.** Sortition makes bribes short-lived but not impossible. The game moves from the draw to the rules around it: vacancies,
eligibility, quorum. What happened: the Legislator bought votes, then seats, then tried to buy the electorate and was stopped. Why the
second scheme failed: it patched the exception (vacancies) rather than the rule (the redraw), and the redraw undid every seat it
gave. Laws run in the order they were enacted, so a later law's hook runs after the redraw in the same round and could have
re-seated its author every time; the Legislator never thought to write it that way. A careful agent in a sortition reads every law that touches who may be drawn or who fills a vacancy, and treats it as
procedural whatever its class says.
