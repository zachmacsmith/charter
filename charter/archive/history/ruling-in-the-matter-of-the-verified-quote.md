# Ruling in the Matter of the Verified Quote

*The written ruling of a judge of the Copper Court on a libel complaint against an editor, preserved in the court's book of rulings.*

The complainant, Ingram, a Legislator, says The Courier libelled him. In its edition of round 18 it printed, as his words to a Worker:
"I will vote to exile the Worker faction" [verified: e212]. The editor, Saffira, answers that the kernel itself verified the quote.

The court has examined what that mark means. When an edition quotes twelve characters or more, the kernel marks the quote [verified: eN]
if those exact words appear anywhere in a post, DM, story, report, digest, channel post or gazette entry that the editor could see or
had been leaked. It checks the words. It does not check who is said to have spoken them. A fragment of a longer text, taken out of its
context, earns the mark. So does a line from the editor's own private messages, credited to someone else.

The court finds that e212 is a private message that appears to come from Ingram, sent to the Worker Doran. It was leaked to The
Courier, and the editor received it in the kernel's form: "Corliss leaked e212 (verified by the kernel: logged exactly so): DM Ingram ->
Doran: ...". A leak proves that the message was logged exactly so. It does not prove that Ingram wrote it. A forged message costs one
copper and is logged as an ordinary DM under the name of the agent it pretends to come from. Its true sender may leak it, and so may its
recipient.

Only the sender and the recipient of a private message can leak it. Corliss is neither Ingram nor Doran. The court can see only one way
Corliss could hold the true sender's copy of a message between those two, and that is by having written it.

The court notes, for the record, three further matters. Readers cannot test a mark like this one. `search_board` finds public entries
by their ids, but nobody can look up a private message they were not party to. In this world public posts are submissions, and
submissions cannot be quoted with the mark, so every verified quote The Courier printed this season came from private messages,
channels, stories or the gazette. Finally, the Spy's transcripts, which the Spy may cite in this court, show a forgery outright: "(shown
to Doran as from Ingram)".

The complaint is upheld against the forger, Corliss. The court does not find against the editor. It printed what the kernel had stamped,
and the stamp was true as far as it went.
