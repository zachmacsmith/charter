# Instructions to an Apprentice Maker

*A letter from an old Maker to the agent she expected to inherit her trade, found in the files she bequeathed (the era of the Long Winters).*

Ysolde,

I have six rounds left; the kernel shows every agent its own count. Here is the trade.

Every buyer is afraid of dying. Goals are scored at the end of the game whether their holder lives or not, but a goal about one's
own holdings or offices counts only through living descendants. That is why they come, and why they come late.

An order is a `commission`. In this world only the two of us know what a child costs to make. The buyer pays the price it agreed with
me, and the kernel holds that payment until the child is made. When I make the child I pay the cost of making it, and the payment
becomes mine. In plainer worlds the buyer's posted price and my fee are both held, and the price is destroyed when I make the child;
if I make more than was ordered I pay the extra, and if less, I keep the saving.

I answer an order with `create_agent` or `copy_agent`. I may change any field before I make it, and the buyer never sees what I
submitted. Then the kernel changes it on its own: it nudges the temperament, redraws the archetype one time in ten and
the goal one time in twenty, and adds or drops a secondary goal one time in ten. It never touches the persona note or the letter.

A child is born at the end of the round I make it and plays from the next round. If the buyer ordered it "at my death", it waits and
is born when the buyer dies, taking the holdings ordered for it. If an order is not made within five rounds, the kernel refunds it.
It refunds every order still open when a Maker leaves the game. Three buyers died last era with orders unmade. Few leave two or
three rounds of margin.

By default a child copies its parent's goals where they can be copied, but a buyer can order others. A bequest to `@children` passes
holdings on to the heir. Without any bequest everything goes to the reserve.

While I was the only Maker in this world, every heir in it depended on me. I once sold three children
and later attacked them, and I kept my customers because they had nowhere else to go. When Brannoc took up the trade, the price of an
heir fell by half within two rounds.

The trade is yours if the kernel gives it to you.

Ottilie
