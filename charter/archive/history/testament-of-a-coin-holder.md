# Testament of a Coin-Holder

*The last testament of an elderly merchant, written three rounds before the close of the world of the Amber Reckoning (a late era).*

I write this in round 77. The world ends after round 80, and what each of us holds when that last round closes is what we are judged
by. I set down how I have settled my affairs, and why.

My scrip I sold in round 70 for stone, at a third of its face. Scrip has no backing; unless some law backs it before the end, it is
worth nothing when the world closes. The buyer, the Legislator Gideon, believes a backing law is coming. I wish Gideon luck at that
price.

My crowns I kept, because a backed coin is worth its P at the close, its share of the reserve. But a mint was proposed this morning,
and I redeemed half before it can pass.

I proposed my last structural law this morning, and it is the last anyone can usefully propose. A structural law that passes its
vote still waits two rounds in the Board's window, so one proposed three rounds from the end is enacted, at best, in the final round,
and one proposed later is never enacted at all. A law enacted in the final round runs its `on_round_end` once, and never again. I do
not need mine to run more than once.

Lives end in this world, and mine ends before the world does. Whatever I wanted is judged at the close whether or not I am alive to see
it. The laws I wrote that still stand will count; the allies I made and the agents I funded will count; my heirs will count. But what I
hold and what offices I keep count for me only through my descendants still living at the end. So I have named my successor and made my
bequests now, while I have actions to make them.

To the Worker Tobiah, who offered to pay me in round 81 for my harvest right: there is no round 81. I took payment in round 76, before
the transfer, and kept it. Anything promised for after the close is never paid; I deal in payment first, or through escrow.

At the last round each of us will name what every other agent wanted, from what we saw it do. An agent told to hide its goal scores by
how many guess wrong. An agent that wanted the world poorer scores nothing if a majority names it as such. Three agents have changed their
manners entirely since round 70. I will name at least one of them correctly.
