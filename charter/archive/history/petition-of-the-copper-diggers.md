# Petition of the Copper Diggers

*A petition from eleven Workers to the Board, with the Board secretary's reply and a later endorsement, kept in the records of the world of the Salt Charter.*

To the Honourable Board. We, the harvesters of the copper camp, ask you to strike two laws.

The first, "Rest for the Copper", set our camp's harvest limit to nothing (`set_harvest_limit(copper, 0)`) "until the stock
recovers". The stock recovered in six rounds; the limit stands. Our rights are held at the copper camp and nowhere else. The
Legislators who passed the law hold theirs at the timber and stone camps, which stay open.

The second charges a "licensing fee" of 3 silver per harvest at our neighbours' camp, paid into the reserve by whoever harvests.
That is a tax, and we were told taxes come before you.

*Reply, from the secretary to the Board.* The Board regrets that it cannot act. Neither law ever came before it. The kernel classes
a law by the words it calls. Closing a camp, capping it and charging at it are camp words (`set_harvest_limit`, `set_quota`,
`set_fee`), and a law made only of camp, naming, text, reading and gazette words is ordinary. Ordinary laws pass by the assembly's
own procedure and never enter the veto window. The Board observes that a fee moves goods as surely as a levy; the kernel classes it
by its word, not its effect.

For completeness the Board adds that renaming anything is ordinary; that a law writing into the gazette, the one text every agent
reads, is ordinary; and that when the Fixer patches an ordinary law into another ordinary law, the patch takes effect at the start
of the next round, also without the Board.

*Endorsement, in another hand, some twenty rounds later.* The petitioners found a Legislator who proposed a procedural law: any
ordinary law that sets a limit or a fee at a camp needs two thirds of the vote. Being procedural, it went to the Board, which let it
through. By then the copper camp had been closed for thirty rounds, and two of the eleven had sold their rights at the stone camp's
price.
