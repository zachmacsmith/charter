# The Quota-Keeper's Notebook

*Working notes of a holder at a shared-price camp, found in a bequeathed file from the Tin Market era.*

There are four of us at camp5. Each of us chooses how much to extract, in whole units up to ten, and the choices are sealed. Everything
we extract sells at one price:

  price = d − Q / Q_sat, never below 2% of d.

Q is the total the four of us extracted. Q_sat is 4 × the number of holders, though never less than 16, so here it is 16. The camp pays
each holder q × price × 4 copper × S/K, where S/K is how full the camp's stock is. We are never told d, the demand. But whenever the price
is above its floor, d = price + Q / Q_sat exactly, so the published total and price give it away. The next round's d is expected at
1 + 0.7 × (d − 1). It drifts back toward one.

The best the four of us can do together is a total of Q_sat × d / 2. At d = 1 that is 8, two each. Then the price is 0.5 and each of us
is paid 2 × 0.5 × 4 = 4 × S/K copper.

Round 12. We agreed on two each. The published total was 11. One of us took 5. I worked it through: the price fell to 1 − 11/16 =
0.3125. Against the quota's 2 × 0.5 = 1, the cheat got 5 × 0.3125 = 1.56, more than one and a half times as much, and each of
the rest of us got 2 × 0.3125 = 0.63 times as much. Only the total and the price are published. Nothing says which of the three others it was.

Round 13. Accusations, denials. Varga swore on her goal. The total was 12.

Round 14. If everyone grabs the maximum, the price falls to its scrap value. I proposed a bond: each of us puts 20 copper into an escrow
law, and if the published total ever goes above the agreed quotas, all four bonds are forfeit. No one is accused and no one can be
cleared, so nobody can hide behind a denial. Two of the others signed, and the third laughed.

Round 15. The total was 11 again. We took that as our answer.

Round 16. The third signed.

Round 22. Totals of exactly 8 for six rounds running. I still don't know who it was.
