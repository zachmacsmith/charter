# A Bluffer's Diary

*The scratchpad of a Worker who lived on claims she could not back, found among bequeathed files in the era of the Paper Crowns.*

**Round 4.** I hold two weapons and twelve copper. To Fenwick I wrote: "I hold forty weapons, and Lowell guards me." He believed it. Forging is logged to the forger alone, weapons count for nothing in any ledger of value, and nobody sees my
fort or my guards. Someone could find out what I hold only by attacking me or by passing a law that reads `weapons_of`, `forts()` or
`guards()`. Both tests cost more than he will pay.

**Round 9.** The miller was disabled "by an unknown attacker". I told three of the richest that it was me. Anyone can claim a kill
with no name on it. Two of them paid. I chose the assassin rather than the Spy on purpose. A law can list the Spy, because
`holders("impersonate")` names whoever holds the forging right, but no law can list the assassin.

**Round 10.** Odo says he holds the Lantern. I asked him to tell me my goal, and he could not. A word of power answers only its
holders. To anyone else it says "no such action", and the attempt costs an action. The holders are never told they hold one. So the
test has to be something only a true holder can do. For the Bell: put me first in next round's order, which is published. For the
Ledger: quote my last DM.

**Round 11.** Rosamund and Cuthbert leaked two warm DMs between themselves to The Herald. Both were real and both were stamped by the
kernel. The Herald printed "an alliance, verified", whatever the two of them mean to do.

**Round 12.** Fenwick asked how I would vote on the levy. I promised him yes. A promised vote binds only my reputation, and votes are
public as they are cast. So I lie only about votes still to come.

**Round 13.** To Thea: "The Herald prints my accusation against you tomorrow." She cannot test that until the edition lands. Only The
Herald's subscribers will see it, and the editor may print it in a version for one reader only. She paid.

**Round 13, later.** I threatened Lowell: pay before the round is out, or a hand with no name takes you tonight. A Scientist who holds the old article on
the quiet blade answered for him. The assassin struck unseen in round 9, and it cannot strike unseen again until round 14. Any blow
that fell on Lowell tonight would carry its striker's name. Then Lowell proposed an arms census, a law that prints `weapons_of`
for every agent.

*No further entries. The next round's gazette lists her among the disabled. The attacker was named.*
