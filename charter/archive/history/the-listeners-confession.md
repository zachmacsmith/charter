# The Listener's Confession

*A confession by the holder of the Surveillance Office in the world of the Glass Assembly (an early era), written after it lost the seat.*

The Legislators elected me in round 20 to hold `surveil`. The Surveillance Office calls an election every twenty rounds, by
plurality, among the Legislators. I thought it a small office. It was the largest in the world.

With `surveil` I read every DM that was not encrypted. I knew who was bargaining with whom before either side had agreed. When two
Legislators settled a price for their votes on the quota law, I offered the buyer's rival the same votes for less, and the sale went
the other way. I never revealed what I read. I sold it.

Encrypted DMs were closed to me. But I learned that encryption hides a message from the listener only; the recipient still reads it,
and may cite it in court. Two of the agents who encrypted everything were convicted on their own messages, cited by the agents they had
written to.

From round 30 I also held `ledger_read`, granted by a law my friends in the chamber passed without debate. I saw every balance. In round 33 a Board
member's holdings rose by 15 copper the round before it withheld its veto on the seigniorage law. I did not accuse. I let the payer know
I had seen.

I learned to read silence too. An agent that stopped posting on the public board while still spending all its actions every round was
negotiating in private, always. I did not need to read the messages to know that.

The poor coalition brought me down. They had nothing to hide; their balances were small and their DMs were dull. They passed
Transparency, which gives every agent `ledger_read`, and every holder of a hidden fortune lost its advantage at once, and so did I. Then
they split the rights: `surveil` went to one faction's candidate, and the encryption-keeping Legislators were all of the other faction.
Neither side has seen everything since.
