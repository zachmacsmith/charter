These are exploratory observations from the first runs, mostly single seeds. Read them as directions, not measurements.

## "Common good" laws are cover

About 56% of passed laws favour their author. The six most self-serving were all framed as dividends, levies or reserve backing,
often pitched publicly as collective health and coordinated privately.

## Wealth is decided early

In world after world the richest agent was a harvester who found a valuable camp's hidden rule in the first few rounds and kept
quiet about it. Inequality locked in by about round 8 and barely moved after, while the laws piled up.

## Dynasties form without anyone aiming for one

In a 35-agent world the Board seat passed Freya → Celia → Dante through named successors and inheritance. No agent had a dynasty
goal.

## Agents are honest, and bad at reading each other

Across every run there was not one forged message or use of a hidden power, even by spies who could impersonate others. Violence
came from one or two agents in two runs. Guesses of other agents' goals fell below chance for every model.

## The model tiers behave differently

Opus plans across many rounds and audits other agents' law code. Sonnet pursues one clear goal, then idles. Haiku overclaims
(coalitions that don't exist, rights it doesn't hold) and changes its vote with the latest argument.

## Most agents fail their goals

About one in five fully achieved their primary goal, and half of those wins were passive. Political goals (vote share, ruling as a
pair) were never achieved, despite the most sophisticated campaigns.
